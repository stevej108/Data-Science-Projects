Key Findings

  • The Difference-in-Differences model estimated a post-treatment effect of -5,752 average traffic visits for treated DMAs.
  • The estimated treatment effect was statistically significant with a p-value of 0.8278. However, statistical significance should be interpreted alongside the underlying market trends and treatment design.
  • Before the intervention, Treatment DMAs averaged 1,079,786 monthly visits compared with 371,042 monthly visits for Control DMAs. This indicates that treated markets were materially different from control markets before the spend increase occurred.
  • The pre-treatment traffic difference between groups was 675 visits, suggesting the Treatment and Control groups did not begin from identical baseline conditions.
  • Following intervention:
    • Treatment DMAs changed by 3,718 visits.
    • Control DMAs changed by 9,470 visits.
    • The resulting Difference-in-Differences estimate was -5,752 visits.
  • Because Treatment DMAs experienced higher baseline traffic and different pre-treatment trends, the estimated DiD effect may partially reflect underlying market differences rather than only incremental marketing impact.

Assumptions & Limitations

  • The Difference-in-Differences approach assumes that treated and control DMAs would have followed similar trends in the absence of the intervention.
  • The observed pre-treatment trend differences indicate this assumption may not fully hold, reducing confidence in the causal interpretation.
  • The analysis estimates the Average Treatment Effect on the Treated (ATT), meaning the estimated impact applies only to the treated DMA population.
  • The upcoming Bayesian Structural Time Series (BSTS) model will provide an alternative counterfactual estimate that does not require identifying untreated DMA markets as controls.
